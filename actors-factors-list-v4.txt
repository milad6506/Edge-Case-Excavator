PART I: UNIVERSAL ACTOR TAXONOMY MODEL & ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

1. UNIVERSAL 3-STATE COMPLIANCE ARCHITECTURE (UNIFORM ACROSS ALL ACTORS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every actor instance evaluated within a scenario is governed by a uniform, 
top-level compliance state machine. Compliant behavior patterns are defined in 
standalone behavioral expectations catalogs (e.g., ADS and Road User expectations) 
and are omitted from this taxonomy catalog to maintain a clean focus on physical 
capabilities and operational misbehaviors.

Top-Level Compliance Attributes:
  * compliance_state: [COMPLIANT | NON_COMPLIANT]
  * non_compliance_type: [NONE | MISUSE | ABUSE]

Definition of Compliance Modes:
  1. COMPLIANT (non_compliance_type = NONE):
     Strict adherence to statutory traffic rules, signals, right-of-way, speed limits,
     and spatial lane boundaries.

  2. MISUSE (non_compliance_type = MISUSE):
     Inadvertent, passive, negligent, or confused non-compliance without active 
     hostile intent to force a collision or assert an illegal right-of-way.
     (e.g., forgotten signal deactivation, inadvertent stop-line overshoot, 
     passive jaywalking on clear roadway, distracted shoulder encroachment).

  3. ABUSE (non_compliance_type = ABUSE):
     Active, aggressive, hostile, or severe statutory violations that actively force 
     surrounding actors or the Ego vehicle into emergency evasive or defensive maneuvers.
     (e.g., running active red lights, running stop signs, unlawful backing/reversing 
     on highways or in tunnels, wrong-way driving, unsignaled cut-in hazards, police 
     impersonation with fake strobes, sudden high-speed pedestrian dart-ins).

2. DIFFERENTIATED MOVEMENT REGIME ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Movement regimes dictate how an actor transitions spatially through the environment:
  * Motor Vehicle Actors (actor_category = MOTOR_VEHICLE):
    Movement regime is driven strictly by the 16 Meta-Actions ([ACT-01] through [ACT-16]).
  * Non-Vehicle Actors (actor_category = VRU | ANIMAL | RAIL_VEHICLE | STATIC_OBSTACLE):
    Movement regime is driven by domain-specific physical trajectory patterns.

3. MISBEHAVIOR REGIME ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Placed directly alongside the movement regime, the misbehavior regime houses all 
specific violation types divided strictly into:
  * misuse_misbehaviors
  * abuse_misbehaviors

1.3 Vehicle Movement Regime (Driven strictly by the 16 Meta-Actions):
  * [ACT-01] Follow Vehicle: Maintain safe longitudinal headway behind lead vehicle.
  * [ACT-02] Accelerate: Increase forward longitudinal velocity lawfully.
  * [ACT-03] Decelerate: Decrease forward longitudinal velocity.
  * [ACT-04] Stop: Stable deceleration to a complete stationary state (v = 0).
  * [ACT-05] Yield: Relinquish right-of-way by decelerating or stopping.
  * [ACT-06] Change Lane: Lateral shift into an adjacent parallel travel lane.
  * [ACT-07] Abort Lane Change: Cancel lateral transition and return to original lane.
  * [ACT-08] Pass: Overtake a slower vehicle via adjacent lane acceleration.
  * [ACT-09] Abort Pass: Cancel overtaking maneuver and return behind lead vehicle.
  * [ACT-10] Turn: Transition between connecting roadways (Left, Right, U-Turn).
  * [ACT-11] Shift Within Lane: Lateral nudge without crossing lane boundaries.
  * [ACT-12] Shift Outside of Lane: Partial or full wheel encroachment over line.
  * [ACT-13] Move Out of Travel Lane/Park: Exit active carriageway to shoulder/curb.
  * [ACT-14] Transition to MRC (Handover): Transfer control to fallback human driver.
  * [ACT-15] Transition to MRC (Autonomous): System executes minimal risk maneuver.
  * [ACT-16] Reverse / Back Up: Longitudinal/lateral control in reverse gear (v < 0).
